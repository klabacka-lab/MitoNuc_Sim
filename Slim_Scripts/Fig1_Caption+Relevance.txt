Caption:
[Fig. 1] [Left] This graph contrasts asexual and sexual populations’ mean fitness level as they recover from mutational meltdown. Both populations recover at a remarkably similar rate, though the sexual population is able to continue the increase to its fitness more than the asexual population. [Right] This histogram compares the mean fitness for the asexual and sexual populations 1000 cycles after the mutational meltdown. The sexual population consistently hit a fitness level of 1.0, while the asexual population varied between 0.985 to approximately 0.999. [Both] Both of these graphs depict the sexual populations ending up with a greater fitness level when compared to the asexual populations.

Relevance:
Our main question is why sex? By comparing how both asexual and sexual populations react and recover from mutational meltdown, it can reveal possible insight. In this simulation’s case, we seed the population with strong deleterious mutations, leaving them with a lower starting fitness level. As the cycles begin, we see the sexual population reaching and maintaining a higher mean fitness level than the asexual. These differences, while small, are statistically significant. While not definitive, these findings provide a possible answer to our question.
